
# Day 14. Borgesian map, Russia: The Map That Became the Territory
![Project overview image](/30_day_map/maps_png/day_14_borgesian.png)
## Overview

- **Theme:** Borgesian Map
- **Project type:** Conceptual cartography and multiscale GIS mapping
- **Study area:** Russia, with a detailed urban inset of Moscow

Inspired by Jorge Luis Borges' short story On Exactitude in Science, this project explores the relationship between cartographic representation and geographical reality. The story imagines an empire where cartographers create a map at a 1:1 scale, making the map as extensive as the territory itself.

The map presents Russia through an intricate combination of geographical features, administrative divisions, settlements, major roads, railways, rivers and lakes. A magnified inset of Moscow reveals a more detailed urban landscape, illustrating how geographical complexity increases as the mapping scale becomes more detailed.

An antique-inspired cartographic design, featuring parchment tones, muted earthy colours and intricate spatial networks, reinforces the conceptual connection between the map and the territory.

## Key takeaways

- The map explores the Borges paradox of cartographic scale and the relationship between geographical reality and its representation.
- The integration of transportation networks, hydrography, settlements and geographical features creates an intricate spatial representation of Russia.
- The Moscow inset demonstrates how increasing spatial detail reveals the complexity of an urban landscape.
- The antique-inspired cartographic design transforms conventional GIS layers into a conceptual map.
- The project highlights an important cartographic idea: even the most detailed map remains an interpretation of the territory rather than the territory itself.
